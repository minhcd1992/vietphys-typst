III. EFFECTS OF SOCRATIC AND AI-MEDIATED
DIALOGUE ON CRITICAL THINKING IN
KINEMATICS
A. Performance on conceptual and quantitative
kinematics problems
The comparative analysis of the six groups revealed a
consistent pattern: across all evaluated situations, Socratic
mediation supported by GAI was associated with system-
atic improvements in response quality for both conceptual
and quantitative kinematics problems. The use of the same
five problems under both instructional conditions enabled
a direct and conceptually aligned comparison of perfor-
mance. Nevertheless, because identical items were reused,
potential practice effects are acknowledged, and the results
are therefore interpreted as evidence of improvement
under the second condition rather than as strong causal
effects.
Before conducting inferential comparisons, the distri-
butional assumptions of the score differences were
examined. A Shapiro–Wilk test, recommended for small
samples, indicated that the overall difference scores were
not compatible with normality (W ¼ 0.736, p ¼ 0.0148,
α ¼ 0.05) [25]. This result, together with the ordinal
nature of the analytic rubric, supported the use of non-
parametric methods in a small-sample context, consistent
with methodological guidance in educational research and
recommendations for cautious interpretation of normality
tests with limited data [26]. Accordingly, the overall
impact of the intervention was evaluated using a one-
tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test, justified by the a priori
directional hypothesis that GAI mediation would not
reduce performance but would instead produce either
improvement or no change. The use of a one-tailed
test was explicitly specified to avoid post hoc inferential
bias [27–29].
Formally, the global null hypothesis H0G stated that the
median of the difference scores between conditions was
less than or equal to zero, that is, that Activity 2 (A2) did
not outperform Activity 1 (A1) in total score. The global
alternative hypothesis H1G stated that the median differ-
ence was greater than zero, indicating that A2 produced
higher scores than A1. The results supported this predic-
tion: H0G was rejected in favor of H1G, with a Wilcoxon
signed-rank statistic of 0 and p ¼ 0.0312 at α ¼ 0.05. The
mean improvement of 8.3 points per group suggests a
consistent effect of the hybrid teacher-GAI mediation,
although reporting the corresponding effect size is rec-
ommended to complete standard statistical reporting [29].
