Recent work further suggests that Socratic dialogue can
be productively extended through the mediation of gen-
erative artificial intelligence (GAI), enabling richer meta-
cognitive engagement and more explicit, evidence-based
argumentation [6]. Such mediation opens new possibilities
for personalized feedback, reflective dialogue, and guided
reconstruction of conceptual errors. While emerging stud-
ies indicate that both human tutors and GAI-based systems
can support critical thinking, empirical evidence within
physics education remains limited. This gap is especially
visible in domains such as one-dimensional kinematics,
where reasoning about motion, representation, and cau-
sality is foundational yet persistently challenging [7,8].
From an epistemic standpoint, GAI can be understood
not merely as an instructional tool but as a form of media-
tion that shapes how learners generate evidence, justify
inferences, and regulate their own reasoning. When
embedded within Socratic dialogue, GAI can function
as an epistemic scaffold by prompting alternative explan-
ations, counterexamples, and multiple solution pathways,
thereby supporting metacognitive monitoring and sense-
making. However, this form of mediation requires careful
pedagogical design to ensure that epistemic agency
remains with the learner rather than being transferred
to the technology itself [9]. Recent scholarship has
emphasized the importance of preserving responsibility,
judgment, and reflective control when AI systems are used
in educational settings [10–12]. Accordingly, the present
study conceptualizes GAI as a supervised epistemic
scaffold intended to support critical thinking and meta-
cognitive regulation, rather than as a replacement for
disciplinary understanding [13].
The preceding discussion situates this study within a
view of physics problem solving that moves beyond
procedural execution toward reasoning, interpretation,
and argumentative sensemaking. Rather than treating
kinematics as an exercise in formula substitution, this
perspective emphasizes the examination of assumptions,
the coordination of representations, and the use of dialogue
to support the development of critical thinking. Within this
framework, Socratic dialogue, when supported by gener-
ative artificial intelligence, offers a means of extending
students’ inferential and metacognitive activity by encour-
aging alternative explanations, conceptual contrasts, and
reflective self-correction.
Guided by this perspective, the present study compares
teacher-guided Socratic dialogue with a subsequent con-
dition in which the same dialogical structure is supported
by generative artificial intelligence. The study therefore
asks
what
differences
in
discipline-specific
critical-
thinking performance are observed between these two
instructional conditions; how participants’ perceptions of
kinematics self-efficacy (KSE), cognitive load (CL), and
intrinsic motivation (IM) differ across the two phases; and
how their qualitative reflections converge with or qualify
the quantitative findings. Because the two conditions were
implemented sequentially in a fixed order, these questions
concern within-sample differences and patterns of con-
vergence rather than the independent causal effect of GAI
mediation. Consequently, this study addresses the follow-
ing research question: what differences emerge in rubric-
assessed critical-thinking performance and qualitative
reasoning when preservice physics teachers solve one-
dimensional kinematics problems through teacher-guided
Socratic dialogue alone, compared with the same dialogue
structure supported by GAI?

II. METHODOLOGY
A. Design study
The present study was conducted using a mixed-
methods approach with a qualitative emphasis and an
interpretive orientation, aimed at examining how critical
thinking develops in initial physics teachers as they engage
in Socratic dialogue while solving conceptual and quanti-
tative problems in one-dimensional kinematics under two
mediation conditions: (1) teacher-guided dialogue without
GAI and (2) teacher-guided dialogue supported by GAI.
This qualitative focus was complemented by a descriptive
and inferential quantitative component that estimated the
magnitude and direction of changes in performance and
learning perceptions across conditions, consistent with
concurrent mixed-methods integration designs described
in the methodological literature [14,15].
An applied empirical design was used to examine
a researcher-developed instructional intervention. The
analysis focuses on both student performance and learning
perceptions associated with a Socratic mediation strategy
implemented either alone or in combination with gener-
ative artificial intelligence within an initial physics teacher
training context. The research adopts a comparative and
explanatory orientation with a practical focus, as it con-
trasts two instructional conditions within the same par-
ticipant group and seeks to interpret observed changes
using qualitative and quantitative evidence, without assert-
ing the strong causal claims associated with randomized or
fully controlled experimental designs. The study employed
a within-group preexperimental repeated-measures design
with two sequential stages using isomorphic tasks. In
