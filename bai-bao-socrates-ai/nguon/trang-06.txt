Given the small sample size (N ¼ 6) and the ordinal
nature of the rubric scores, a nonparametric approach
was retained for all problem-level contrasts in order to
avoid unstable analytical decisions based on low-power
normality tests [26].
Subsequently, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with two-
tailed contrasts were applied separately for each problem,
as summarized in Table III. This analysis enabled paired
comparisons of scores obtained in Activity 1 (A1) and
Activity 2 (A2) at the problem level, reporting the medians
for each condition as well as the median of the difference
scores (A2 −A1). From a descriptive perspective, a con-
sistent directional pattern is observed: across all five
problems, the median score in A2 exceeds that in A1,
with median differences ranging from 1.0 to 3.5 points.
From an inferential standpoint, at α ¼ 0.05, statistically
significant differences were identified for Problems 1 (P1)
and 4 (P4), whereas for Problems 2 (P2), 3 (P3), and 5
(P5), the results did not reach the threshold for statistical
significance, despite exhibiting the same direction of
improvement.
For each problem k, hypotheses were formulated as
follows for the two-tailed tests. The null hypothesis H0k
stated that the median of the score differences between A2
and A1 was equal to zero, indicating no systematic change
between conditions. The alternative hypothesis H1k stated
that the median difference was different from zero,
indicating a systematic change between conditions. The
choice of a two-tailed contrast at the problem level is
justified as a methodologically conservative approach and
is aligned with standard reporting practices when multiple
comparisons are conducted and when unwarranted direc-
tional testing without a strict and explicitly declared a priori
theoretical constraint is to be avoided—a practice widely
cautioned against in the methodological literature due to its
potential to bias inference [28,29].
However, when the theoretical framework specifies
a priori a clear directional expectation—namely, that
mediation supported by generative artificial intelligence
should not result in performance detriment and that any
observable change, if present, would be in the direction
of improvement—the use of a one-tailed contrast can be
defended, provided that this directionality is declared
before data inspection and reported transparently. Under
this condition, for each problem k, the null hypothesis H0k
is defined as the median of the difference scores being less
than or equal to zero, whereas the alternative hypothesis
H1k is defined as the median being greater than zero. This
formulation focuses inference on the theoretically relevant
direction of change while reducing the risk of opportun-
istic reinterpretation of results [27–29].
