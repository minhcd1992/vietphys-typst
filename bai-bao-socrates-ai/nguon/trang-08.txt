efficient visual inference of performance shifts across
problems and groups [31,32].
Taken together, the findings support a coherent and
convergent empirical narrative across levels of analysis. At
the global level, the alternative hypothesis H1G received
statistical support, indicating a significant improvement
in performance when Socratic instruction incorporated
mediation by GAI. At the problem level, the alternative
hypothesis H1k was supported for two of the five problems,
while the remaining three exhibited a homogeneous
directional pattern favoring Activity 2 (A2). In studies
with small sample sizes, this pattern is methodologically
informative, as consistent directionality across multiple
comparisons can be compatible with a stable underlying
effect even when statistical significance is not reached
in all individual contrasts, particularly under limited
power and conservative testing strategies at the problem
level [26,29].
In addition, no negative changes were identified for any
group or problem, suggesting that the incorporation of
GAI did not produce observable performance detriment
within the examined framework. However, this absence of
decreases should be interpreted cautiously: it does not
constitute inferential evidence of “no harm,” but rather
indicates that no systematic declines were observed under
the present sample and conditions.
Taken together, the evidence integrated across Tables III
and IVand Fig. 2 supports the claim that hybrid human-AI
mediation can strengthen processes associated with critical
thinking in problem solving, particularly by promoting
more precise explanations, explicit verification of assump-
tions, and metacognitive self-regulation during reasoning,
provided that teacher supervision and explicit criteria for
response validation are maintained [33,34].
B. Quantitative analysis of shared perception
dimensions across instructional conditions
To characterize the learning experience under both
Socratic
instructional
modalities—teacher-guided
and
teacher-GAI-mediated—perception surveys were admin-
istered to assess dimensions common to Activities 1 (A1)
and 2 (A2). The dimensions evaluated were KSE, CL, and
IM. Comparison across these shared dimensions indicates
that the transition toward hybrid teacher-GAI mediation
did not merely represent a change of tool, but rather a
modification in how prospective physics teachers experi-
enced the task.
For KSE, a clear increase was observed. Overall
agreement rose from 84.7% to 94.4%, responses in the
“Disagree” category disappeared, and the proportion of
“Strongly agree” responses increased from 26.4% to
37.5%. This pattern suggests enhanced confidence in
understanding and solving kinematics problems under
the GAI-mediated condition. The response pattern for
CL was more heterogeneous. Overall agreement increased
modestly from 59.7% to 65.3%, yet some items exhibited
polarization, with responses clustering at opposite posi-
tions. This indicates that, for certain items, part of the
group reported higher mental demand, while others
reported no increase or even a reduction in perceived
effort. For instance, agreement on item CL1 increased from
77.8% to 88.9%, indicating that several participants
perceived greater attentional effort. At the same time,
others described a redistribution of effort, associated with
verification activities and quality control of responses
generated by the GAI. Finally, IM exhibited the most
pronounced effect: agreement reached 100% in A2, com-
pared to an initial 87.5%, eliminating all neutral and
disagreement responses.
