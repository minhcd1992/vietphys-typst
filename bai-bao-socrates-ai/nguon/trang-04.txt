C. The SOCRATIC-IA socratic learning model
The instructional intervention was implemented through
the SOCRATIC-IA Socratic learning model, a framework
developed by the authors to promote discipline-specific
critical thinking during the solution of conceptual and
quantitative problems in one-dimensional kinematics. The
model integrates three instructional components: (i) con-
ceptual activation, (ii) teacher-guided Socratic dialogue,
and (iii) teacher-guided Socratic dialogue mediated by
GAI. This structure allows the evolution of students’
argumentative and metacognitive reasoning to be exam-
ined under two levels of epistemic mediation while
keeping the disciplinary content and problem-solving
sequence constant.
The complete instructional sequence was organized into
three stages and implemented over 10 h. A 2-h initial stage
was devoted to activating foundational kinematics con-
cepts and aligning conceptual criteria across participants.
This was followed by two 4-h stages in which students
engaged in structured Socratic problem solving, first with
teacher mediation alone and then with teacher mediation
supported by GAI. Table I summarizes the instructional
structure of the SOCRATIC-IA model, outlining the three
stages of the intervention, their pedagogical purposes, the
core learning activities, and the corresponding assessment
strategies used in each stage.
The problem worksheets included conceptual and quan-
titative tasks designed to elicit interpretation, justification,
comparison of alternative explanations, and explicit veri-
fication of the physical principles involved. As a repre-
sentative example, participants were asked to solve the
task, which required students to identify the relevant
kinematic principles, explain the relationships among
the variables, justify the selected solution pathway, and
evaluate the conceptual consistency of the final response.
The complete problem statement, accompanying figure,
and Socratic prompts are provided in Appendix in
Supplementary Material.

D. Ethical considerations and validity
of the SOCRATIC-IA model
The present study was conducted in accordance with the
ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki [23]. All
participants provided written informed consent after being
informed about the objectives of the study, the voluntary
nature of participation, their right to withdraw at any time,
and the procedures used to ensure confidentiality and data
protection. These safeguards are particularly important in
educational research involving emerging technologies,
where transparency and the protection of participants’
rights are essential [24].
